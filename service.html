01

LSTS × Fu Jen Catholic University

Where · Kon Tum, Vietnam 2024 · Programming teaching team 2025 · Vice Camp Leader & Media lead

Teaching Scratch to students who had never used a computer for making things.

Two camps in Kon Tum, roughly 200 elementary and middle school students between them. I taught Scratch, English, PowerPoint and presentation skills — and learned that explaining something is a completely different skill from knowing it.

What changed my approach

My first lesson plan was too good.

I prepared the material I would have wanted: efficient, complete, logically ordered. It was technically correct and it did not work. The students weren't stuck on the concepts — they were stuck on the gap between a block on a screen and a thing that happens because of them.

So we rebuilt the sessions around getting something moving in the first ten minutes, then explaining why afterwards. That is the same lesson the field kept teaching me in robotics: you don't get to decide whether your design is good. The person using it does.

A solution can be technically correct and still be a bad experience for the person it was built for.

  • 2024 · Coding Summer Camp. Programming teaching team — Scratch and digital skills, working alongside LSTS and Fu Jen students and a professor.
  • 2025 · Career Summer Camp. Vice Camp Leader — co-led camp operations, ran the Media Team, and worked as translator between the LSTS and Fu Jen groups.
02

LSTS Robotics Club

2024–2025 · Member 2025–2026 · Club President Result · Best Club of the Year

Twenty-three people, one lab, four workshops.

I joined this club as someone who wanted to build robots. I ended up running it — which turned out to be a different problem entirely, and mostly not a technical one.

What the job actually was

Operations, project planning, competition preparation, and splitting work across mechanical design, programming, strategy and testing. Four workshops in the year — two for students, two for parents — and about twenty younger members to mentor through their first builds.

What I got wrong first

I assumed leading meant doing the hardest tasks myself. It meant the opposite: making sure someone less experienced could take a task, get stuck, and still finish it. A club where one person builds everything is a club with one engineer in it.

That year the club was named Best Club of the Year for the first time. I'd like to say it was a strategy. Really it was the result of a lot of people finally having something specific to do.

03

Vietnam Robotics Ambassador

From · June 2026 Role · Innovation Dept. Leader, Southern Region Team · FIRST Global Challenge Team Vietnam

Robotics turns out to travel better than language does.

Selected as one of around twenty Team Vietnam representatives from an initial cohort of about a hundred candidates, after training, interviews and evaluation — heading to the 2026 FIRST Global Challenge in Incheon, Republic of Korea.

Open STEM Day

Led the Southern Region Innovation Department for a day reaching roughly 50 primary, middle and high school students at FPT Hậu Giang School.

Think Bootcamp

A smaller, deeper programme for around 10 Southern-region students, combining fire-prevention STEM workshops with hands-on robotics.

Exchange

Co-hosted four technical and cultural exchange meetings with teams from Taiwan, France, Türkiye and Palestine.

Logistics

Assembled demonstration robots and helped secure project funding and hardware from Maker Viet.

Why this matters to me

In an exchange session with a team you share no language with, you find out very quickly how much of engineering communication is just a robot on a table and two people pointing at it. The mechanism explains itself. Everything else — why you chose it, what it cost, what you'd do differently — is the part you have to work for.

That's also why I care about the human side of the systems I want to build. It stopped being an abstract idea somewhere between a classroom in Kon Tum and a pit table in Hsinchu.
