01

Fridgi · LSTS F.E.A.T. Week

May 2026 Role · Software & hardware developer · Business strategy Result · Most Excellent Project, Grade 11

A container that can smell fruit going bad.

Fruit doesn't spoil quietly — it releases ethylene as it ripens. Fridgi is an ESP32-based smart container that reads that signal, estimates where the fruit is in its ripening curve, and tells you before you find out by opening the fridge.

The build

Sensors, filtration, and a number people can act on.

  • Sensing. Gas/VOC and humidity sensors inside the container, feeding an ESP32.
  • Filtration. An active micro-fan filtration stage, so the system can act on what it measures instead of only reporting it.
  • Logic. Real-time monitoring that accounts for environmental noise and for how much fruit is actually in the box — a reading means nothing without knowing the volume producing it.
  • Output. Automated storage management plus smartphone alerts, because a sensor nobody reads is not a product.
  • Cost. Technical blueprints and a bill of materials targeting a per-unit BOM of roughly US$5, to keep mass production realistic.

What interested me wasn't adding AI or sensors. It was starting from a real problem and asking what technology could actually contribute.

The hardest part wasn't the electronics — it was the signal. Ethylene concentration depends on how much fruit is in the container, how long it's been closed, and what else is in the room. Reading a number is easy. Deciding what that number means is the engineering.

Problem

Household fruit spoils before anyone notices, and "check it yourself" clearly isn't working.

Approach

Measure the gas that ripening already produces, rather than asking users to log anything.

Build

ESP32 + gas/VOC and humidity sensing + active micro-fan filtration + mobile alerts.

Business

B2C hardware and B2B supermarket SaaS pricing models, projecting 50–60% gross margins.

Outcome

Most Excellent Project for Grade 11, and presented to a visiting delegation from Taiwan.

02

VEX V5 VR Coding Online Challenge

2025–2026 Discipline · Autonomous programming Result · 3rd worldwide

No metal. No excuses.

In simulation nothing is loose, nothing is bent, and no bracket is one inch too high. Every point you don't score is a decision you got wrong — which makes it the purest feedback loop in the whole sport.

What it rewarded

Route planning, sequencing, and knowing exactly what the robot should be doing in every second of the run. There's no driver to recover from a bad plan, so the plan has to be right, and then the plan has to be optimised — repeatedly, against a scoreboard that updates worldwide.

What it gave back

This is where the Vietnam Signature Event method came from. Plan, simulate, adapt — I'd already spent a season doing exactly that in VR before we needed it on a real field with almost no time left.

Ranked 3rd in the world — and it's the project that taught me to treat a route like a design, not a script.

03

Engineering notebook

2024 – present Role · Contributor and maintainer Recognition · Create Award, Nationals 2026

The record of everything I believed and was wrong about.

The notebook isn't documentation written after the fact. It's the only place a rebuild argument survives contact with four people who all liked the old robot.

What goes in it

  • The decision, not just the outcome. What we chose, what we rejected, and the reason at the time — including reasons that later looked stupid.
  • Measurements. Cycle times, failure counts, which match a part broke in. Numbers end arguments that opinions extend.
  • Dead ends. The mechanisms that didn't work are more useful six months later than the ones that did.
  • One change at a time. The pit rule from Taiwan, written down so it survives the pressure that makes people abandon it.

At the 2026 National Championship this is what earned the Create Award — recognition for the engineering process rather than the day's scores. It's the result I'm most attached to, because it's the only one that judges how I think rather than how the robot performed on one Saturday.
