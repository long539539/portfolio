About me

A loader one inch too high.

We kept losing autonomous runs, and I was certain the code was wrong. We rewrote the routine. It still missed. The loader was about one inch too high — a mechanical problem no amount of programming was ever going to fix.

That was the moment robotics stopped being a coding hobby for me. I had joined the school robotics club in Grade 10 because building things looked fun. What I found instead was a system where a bracket, a sensor position and a line of C++ all fail together — and where the honest answer to "why isn't this working" is almost never the first answer you reach for.

How do you design a machine where structure, control and decision-making reinforce each other instead of compensating for each other?

— the question I've been chasing ever since

What I built

Two seasons of competition robots: a first-year High Stakes machine we built straight from sketches, and a Push Back season where the same robot was rebuilt three times — basket bot, then a completely different S-bot, then a machine we stopped touching with a wrench and started rebuilding in software.

What I learned

Watching stronger teams in Taiwan changed how I work. They treated a working robot as a temporary version, read match data as engineering evidence, and rebuilt major mechanisms when results said the concept was wrong. I used to protect my designs. Now I try to falsify them.

Where I'm going

I want to work on humanoid robots and autonomous mobile manipulation — machines that operate somewhere less predictable than a 12-by-12 field. I can already tune a system until it behaves. What I want next is to understand why the behaviour changed.

The arc

One season, four robots.

The 2025–2026 Push Back season is the spine of this portfolio. Five competitions, and a different engineering problem behind each one.

Chapter 01 · SSIS Scrimmage

Building a basket bot from a blank page.

First contact with Push Back. We designed in CAD before cutting metal for the first time — and found out how much a scrimmage teaches you that a workshop can't.

Tournament Finalist · 3rd in quals
Chapter 02 · Southern Regional

The rebuild we didn't want to do.

The scrimmage exposed a cycle time we couldn't fix by tuning. So we took the robot apart and changed the intake path.

Judges Award
Chapter 03 · Taiwan Signature Event

Throwing away a working robot.

A completely different concept — basket bot to S-bot — and the first time I competed against teams that rebuild faster than we iterate.
